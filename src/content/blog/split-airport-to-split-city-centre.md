Split Airport (SPU) sits in Kaštela, roughly 25 kilometres west of the city of Split. After a long flight, the last thing most travellers want is to spend time working out how to reach their accommodation. This guide walks through the realistic options for getting from the terminal into the Split city centre, so you can decide before you land.

## How far is the airport from the centre?

The drive between Split Airport and central Split is usually around 30 to 40 minutes, depending on traffic. In July and August the coastal approach into the city can be slower, especially in the late morning and around ferry departure times, so it is worth allowing extra time if you have an onward connection.

## Option 1: The airport shuttle bus

There is a shuttle bus service that runs between the airport and the main bus station near the Split ferry port, timed loosely around flight arrivals. It is the cheapest way into town and drops you close to the Riva waterfront and the old town.

The trade-offs are practical ones. You may wait for the next scheduled departure, the bus makes a fixed stop rather than taking you to your door, and you then have to walk or take onward transport with your luggage. If you are staying inside the pedestrian old town, or further out in a neighbourhood like Žnjan or Bačvice, the final stretch is on you.

## Option 2: Public city buses

Local Promet city buses also serve the airport area and connect toward Split via Kaštela and Solin. This is the budget choice, but it involves more stops, possible changes, and limited luggage space. It works best for light travellers who are comfortable navigating a local network, and far less well for families with suitcases or anyone arriving late at night.

## Option 3: Taxi from the rank

Taxis wait outside the terminal. A taxi takes you door to door without a fixed timetable, which is convenient. The downside is price uncertainty: fares to the centre vary, and at peak times the rank can have a queue. If you take a taxi, it is sensible to confirm the approximate fare before you set off.

## Option 4: A pre-booked private transfer

A private transfer is arranged in advance, so a driver is expecting your specific flight. You send your destination, arrival date and time, the number of passengers and your luggage, and you receive a fixed quote by email before you travel. There are no surprises about the price, and the vehicle is matched to your group — a car for a couple, or a van or minibus for a larger party.

The main advantages are predictability and convenience. You are taken directly to your accommodation, including help finding the nearest drop-off point if you are staying inside the pedestrian old town, where cars cannot reach every address. For families, groups, late arrivals and anyone connecting to a ferry, this removes a lot of the friction from arrival day.

## Which should you choose?

A few simple questions usually point to the right answer:

- **Travelling light and on a budget, flexible on timing?** The shuttle bus is hard to beat on price.
- **Want the cheapest possible option and happy to navigate?** A local city bus works.
- **Arriving without a plan and want door-to-door now?** A taxi from the rank is straightforward.
- **Want a known price, a driver waiting for your flight, and a vehicle sized for your group or family?** A pre-booked private transfer is the most predictable.

## A note on the old town

Much of Split's old town sits inside a UNESCO-protected, pedestrian-only core built around Diocletian's Palace. Cars cannot drive to every doorstep there. Whichever option you choose, it helps to know the name of your accommodation and the nearest vehicle-accessible street. With a private transfer you can share the accommodation name in advance so the driver can plan the closest practical drop-off.
