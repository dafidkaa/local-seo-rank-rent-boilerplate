Arriving in Split with children adds a few moving parts to the start of any holiday: car seats, strollers, more luggage, and the simple reality that tired kids do not enjoy waiting around after a flight. The good news is that a little planning makes the airport-to-accommodation leg one of the smoothest parts of the trip. Here is what families travelling to the Split area should think about.

## Child seats and the law

Croatia, like the rest of the EU, requires that young children travel in an appropriate child or booster seat suited to their age and size. This applies to transfers and taxis, not just your own car at home. Rather than wrestling your own bulky seats through the airport, you can request child and booster seats as part of a private transfer.

The key is to tell the operator the **number and ages of the children** when you send your inquiry, so the right seats are prepared in advance. Ages matter because an infant, a toddler and a six-year-old each need different equipment. The more specific you are, the better the fit on arrival.

## Right-sizing the vehicle

Families almost always carry more than they expect: suitcases, a stroller, a travel cot, beach gear and the inevitable extra bag. A standard car that looks fine for the headcount can run out of boot space fast.

Think in terms of both seats and luggage:

- **Two adults and one or two small children:** a comfortable car is usually enough, but check there is room for the stroller.
- **A family of four or five with full luggage:** a van is often the easier choice, with space for everyone plus bags and a cot.
- **Two families travelling together, or a multi-generation group:** a minibus keeps everyone in one vehicle rather than splitting across taxis.

When you request a quote, list your luggage honestly — including pushchairs and any bulky items — so the vehicle is matched to what you are actually bringing.

## Timing around little ones

A few habits make arrival day calmer:

- **Pre-book so a driver meets your flight.** Avoiding a queue at the taxi rank with restless children is worth a lot.
- **Keep essentials reachable.** Snacks, water, wipes and a change of clothes should be in a daypack, not buried in a checked suitcase.
- **Allow margin for connections.** If you are continuing to a ferry or a more distant town, build in a buffer for a late flight rather than racing the clock with kids in tow.

## Door-to-door to a villa or apartment

Many families staying near Split book a villa or apartment rather than a hotel, often on Čiovo, in Podstrana or along the Omiš Riviera. These addresses can be tucked away on small roads that buses do not serve well. A private transfer that goes directly to the door means no final leg dragging suitcases and a stroller, and no working out an unfamiliar local bus with tired children. Share the address when you inquire so the driver can plan the route.

## A note on safety and honesty

Child and booster seats can be arranged on request, but they should always be requested in advance — they are not something to assume will simply appear. By giving clear details about your children's ages and your luggage up front, you let the operator prepare properly, which is the whole point of booking ahead.

## Make arrival the easy part

Holidays with children have enough variables. The transfer from Split Airport does not need to be one of them. With the right vehicle, the correct seats prepared in advance, and a driver expecting your flight, you can step off the plane and head straight to the pool.
