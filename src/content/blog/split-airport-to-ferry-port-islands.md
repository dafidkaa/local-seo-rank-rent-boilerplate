For many travellers, Split Airport is not the final destination — it is the first step toward Hvar, Brač, Vis, Šolta or Korčula. The connection runs through Split's ferry port, where catamarans and car ferries depart for the central Dalmatian islands. Because ferry timetables are fixed and summer crowds are real, getting this connection right matters more than almost any other part of the journey.

## Where the boats leave from

Split Ferry Port is on the eastern side of the city harbour, right next to the main bus and train stations and a short walk from the Riva. Jadrolinija and other operators run from here, with a mix of passenger-only catamarans and larger car ferries. Catamarans are faster and more limited in capacity; car ferries are slower but take vehicles and rarely sell out for foot passengers in the same way.

The airport is about 25 kilometres away, so you need to budget for the transfer plus a comfortable margin at the port itself.

## How much time to allow

This is the part people underestimate. A realistic plan looks like this:

- **Transfer time:** usually 30 to 45 minutes from the airport, but allow more in peak summer when the approach to the port is busy.
- **At the port:** arrive at least 30 minutes before a catamaran, and longer for a car ferry, especially if you are bringing a vehicle or need to collect pre-booked tickets.
- **Buffer for the flight:** flights can land late. If your boat is the last departure of the day to your island, build in extra cushion or plan to overnight in Split rather than risk missing it.

If your flight arrives in the early evening, check the last sailing time to your island carefully before assuming you will make it.

## Getting from the airport to the port

You have the same broad options as any airport arrival, but the time pressure changes the calculation:

- **Shuttle or city bus:** cheapest, but tied to a timetable and a fixed stop, and you still walk to the catamaran berth with your bags. Cutting it fine here is risky.
- **Taxi:** door to port, no timetable, but fare and queue can be uncertain at peak times.
- **Pre-booked private transfer:** a driver waits for your specific flight and takes you straight to the port with space for all your luggage. For a time-sensitive ferry connection, the predictability is the main value — you are not waiting for the next scheduled bus or hunting for a taxi after a delayed landing.

## Tickets and luggage

Buy island ferry tickets in advance where you can, particularly for popular catamaran routes to Hvar in high season, which can sell out. Keep your tickets and passports easy to reach rather than buried in a suitcase. If you are travelling as a group or family with a lot of luggage, a single van or minibus transfer keeps everyone and their bags together and avoids splitting across multiple taxis.

## A smoother island connection

The islands are the reward, but the connection day is where trips go sideways. A little planning — knowing your boat's departure time, leaving margin for a late flight, and arranging transport that meets your flight rather than a timetable — turns a tense dash into an easy hop.
